Experience

My work has moved between health-policy evaluation, global health estimation, and business research, but the problem has always been the same: turning complex data into estimates that people can trust and use. Teaching is where I practice explaining those methods clearly.

Work Experience

Economist — Adolescent Mental Health Study, CHOICE Institute, University of Washington

Sep 2026 – Present

Supervisor: Noémi Kreif

At the CHOICE Institute, I work with Noémi Kreif on a study of how depression in adolescence shapes adult working lives: hours, labor-force participation, earnings, and education. The study uses restricted-use Add Health data and a survey-weighted, cross-fitted targeted learning (TMLE) design.

My role sits between the econometrics and the economics. I review the causal design and how outcomes are measured, audit the R estimation pipeline, and translate treatment effects into labor-supply elasticities that feed downstream cost and return-on-investment models. Along the way I have documented identification threats and coding errors, proposed diagnostics and sensitivity analyses, and re-estimated 17 outcomes in the public NLSY97 with cross-fitted AIPW, giving the team a public-data benchmark for its restricted-data results.

Data Scientist Researcher — HIV Subnational Estimation, Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME)

Summer 2025

At the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation, I produced subnational estimates of HIV prevalence, antiretroviral-therapy coverage, and viral-load suppression for Malawi and Zimbabwe. The work meant combining several national surveys (PHIA, DHS, and AIS) into one consistent pipeline, running statistical, machine-learning, and forecasting analyses, and documenting legacy code so the end-to-end workflow could be maintained and reused.

Research Assistant — Smith School of Business, Queen's University

May 2021 – Aug 2021

As a research assistant at the Smith School of Business, I integrated and cleaned data from multiple sources, validated its quality, and communicated statistical findings through reproducible analysis in R and Stata, habits I still rely on in my research today.
