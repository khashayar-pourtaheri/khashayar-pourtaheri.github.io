About

Khashayar Pourtaheri

I'm a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Washington, advised by Alan Griffith, and currently on the job market.

My research uses causal inference to answer policy questions in health, labor, and crime. In my job market paper, I estimate what society gains and loses when states reduce criminal penalties for drug possession: in Oregon and Washington, the net social cost reached $26.6 billion by mid-2023, mostly from excess overdose deaths. With Ahmad Zareei, I also run experiments with LLM personas and show that they predict a person's decisions much better when given that person's past choices than when given any description of who they are.

I came to economics from mechanical engineering and completed a master's at Queen's University before starting my PhD. I use administrative, survey, and panel data in R, Python, and Stata, and I care about analysis that others can check and build on. Alongside my own projects, I work as an economist at UW's CHOICE Institute and have taught introductory microeconomics as an instructor and data science labs as a teaching assistant.
