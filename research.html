Job market paper

The Social Cost of Drug Decriminalization

SSRN working paper, October 2026 · Paper (SSRN)

What does society gain and lose when states reduce criminal penalties for drug possession? Using FBI arrest and crime records and CDC death records, I compare Oregon's and Washington's 2021 reforms with weighted comparison states using synthetic difference-in-differences. Drug-user arrests fall 68 percent, while overdose deaths rise 34 percent and property crime 17 percent. Restoring penalties in Washington brings arrests back but not deaths down. The net social cost reaches $26.6 billion by mid-2023, mostly from 1,971 excess deaths.

Working paper

Behavioral History Outperforms Descriptions of the Person for LLM Synthetic Personas

With Ahmad Zareei · arXiv preprint, October 2026 · Paper (arXiv)

We build 4,225 synthetic personas with Claude, one for each of 845 U.S. adults under five nested information conditions, and test which information predicts each person's choices on 14 behavioral biases. Demographics, personality, and cognitive scores, even combined, reach only 7–12 percent of the human test–retest benchmark; adding the person's earlier answers raises this to 28 percent, the best in all 17 demographic groups. Population averages look right in every condition but hide individual-level failure.
